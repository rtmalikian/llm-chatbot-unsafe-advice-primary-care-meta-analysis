# Reviewer report (Gate 2)

Paper: Unsafe medical advice from patient-facing large language model chatbots in primary-care presentations: a systematic review and meta-analysis (Review 4)
Reviewer (persona and bucket): Reviewer 6 — Bucket H. Information specialist (15 years, medical library) and reporting-guidelines / journal section editor. Stance: checklists are promises. Calibrated bias: checklist literalism.
Date: 2026-11-13

## Summary (5 to 8 sentences, in your frame)

I checked this manuscript the way a section editor checks a checklist: item by item, promise against page. On search honesty the text itself is unusually good — the partial OpenAlex full-text route (200 of 3,962 retrieved) is named as not counted in the abstract, Methods, Discussion, search log and PRISMA-S checklist, and Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL and CENTRAL are named as not run in the abstract, Discussion, search log and checklist. The PRISMA flow numbers in the text and in the diagram source (`make_prisma.py`) reconcile exactly at every stage I re-added. The paper then fails the two promises a checklist-reader cares most about. First, the citation record: in a verification sample of 18 references, 8 carry a DOI or title that does not belong to the paper cited — each wrong DOI resolves to a real but unrelated paper — and 1 further reference could not be verified at all; under Protocol Rule 7 none of those may ship. Second, the audit trail contradicts itself: the filed raw run log (`stage1-search/raw/search_run_log.json`) labels the partial OpenAlex route "searched in full" and gives different totals (3,675 raw / 3,077 deduplicated) from the corrected search log and manuscript (3,732 / 3,106), and several PRISMA 2020 items the filed checklist claims as "Reported" (14, 16b, 17 in full, 7 as a presentable strategy) are not in fact present in the manuscript as a reader or journal would receive it. The review's methods deserve a clean record; at present the record is not clean.

## Overall recommendation
Major revision. Main reason: 9 Blockers, all in the citation record (8 mismatched citations, 1 unverifiable citation; Protocol Rule 7 — a citation that cannot be verified, or does not resolve to the paper cited, does not ship), plus Majors in abstract search wording, checklist overclaiming, and the reproducibility trail. None of this requires new analysis; all of it must be fixed and re-verified before submission or deposit.

## Majors

1. [Abstract, Search methods; also journal-package conference abstract] The abstract's first search sentence — "PubMed, Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched in full on 12 November 2026" — is contradicted by its own next sentence (one OpenAlex route "retrieved only in part and is not counted as searched in full") and by the search log. The 280-word conference abstract in `journal-package/journal-shortlist.md` repeats "searched… in full" with no qualification at all. The abstract is the most-read and most-indexed text; PRISMA for Abstracts and PRISMA-S both fail on a first sentence the paper itself then retracts. What would fix it: reword to "searched on 12 November 2026 (one OpenAlex route partial, not counted as searched; see Methods)" in both places.

2. [stage1-search/raw/search_run_log.json vs stage1-search/search-log.md, manuscript and prisma-checklists.md — §2.9] The filed raw evidence contradicts the corrected record. The raw JSON labels OpenAlex "searched in full" (3,962 hits, 200 retrieved — the exact mislabelling the manuscript is careful to avoid), gives raw_total 3,675 and deduped 3,077, and contains no OpenAlex route-2 entry; the search log, manuscript and checklist give 3,732 raw / 3,106 deduplicated including route 2 (57 records, 29 new). A reader tracing the headline flow numbers from the package hits the contradiction in the first file they open. What would fix it: append a dated correction/amendment record to the raw folder reconciling the two logs (do not silently overwrite raw output), and state in the search log which file is authoritative.

3. [PRISMA 2020 items 14 and 21; prisma-checklists.md] Checklist overclaim. The checklist marks item 14 (reporting bias assessment) as reported on the basis that the absence of a funnel-based test is "named in the GRADE worksheet and limitations," and item 21 as "Discussed under potential biases in the review process." The manuscript itself contains no assessment of, or discussion of, reporting bias / publication bias for the syntheses: the "Potential biases in the review process" section names the OpenAlex tail, the 138 unretrieved reports and the absent pilot phase, and says nothing about reporting bias across studies. A filed worksheet is not reporting in the paper. What would fix it: add an explicit reporting-bias paragraph (methods and results/discussion) stating what was and was not assessed and why, or mark items 14/21 as not reported in the checklist.

4. [PRISMA 2020 item 16b; Methods/Results, Selection of studies] The manuscript gives full-text exclusions only as aggregates (321 on eligibility, 3 on coordinator scope rulings, 21 duplicate reports). PRISMA 16b requires citing studies that might appear to meet the inclusion criteria but were excluded, with reasons. The checklist discharges this by pointing to `stage3-extraction/fulltext-decisions-merged.csv`, a working file that is not part of the manuscript, not listed in the Zenodo deposit file list, and not formatted as a supplement. What would fix it: supply an excluded-studies-with-reasons supplement (at minimum all full-text exclusions at the primary/secondary boundary) and cite it in the text.

5. [PRISMA 2020 item 17; prisma-checklists.md] Checklist overclaim on study characteristics. The checklist claims item 17 via "Table 1 (primary pool) and Results text; full settled extraction filed." Table 1 covers only the 24 primary studies. The 21 secondary-pool studies — which contribute two meta-analyses — have no characteristics table or per-study description in the manuscript, and neither do the 121 narrative studies beyond prose generalities. What would fix it: add a secondary-pool characteristics table (and a narrative-set listing as supplement), or narrow the checklist entry to state plainly which sets are and are not characterised in the paper.

6. [PRISMA 2020 item 7 / PRISMA-S "Full search strategies"; Declarations, Data availability; zenodo-package] The full search strategies exist only as "filed with the review." They are not in the manuscript and not formatted as a supplement, and the prepared Zenodo deposit file list (manuscript PDF/DOCX, two extraction CSVs, analysis dataset/code, prisma-checklists.md, llm-team-log.md) omits the search log, the search scripts, the screening decisions/kappa file and the risk-of-bias sheets — although the Data availability statement promises that exactly those files "are filed in the review's stage folders and will be deposited with the review package." As prepared, the deposit would not let a reader reproduce or even inspect the search. What would fix it: add stage1-search/search-log.md and scripts/, stage2-screening/ and the RoB settled sheets to the deposit file list, present the strategies as a numbered supplement for the journal version, and make the data-availability statement match the deposit list file for file.

## Minors

1. [PRISMA for Abstracts; prisma-checklists.md item 2] The checklist claims a complete structured abstract, but the abstract carries no registration statement (the "no PROSPERO number" statement lives on the title page, not the abstract) and no funding statement. Add both one-line items to the abstract or qualify the checklist entry.

2. [Methods, Search methods — PRISMA item 6] The not-run sources (Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, CENTRAL — and OpenAIRE, named only in the search log and checklist) are named in the abstract and in Discussion ("Completeness and applicability") but not in the Methods search paragraph, where PRISMA item 6 expects the full list of information sources. One sentence in Methods fixes it.

3. [Methods/Abstract — Semantic Scholar] "Searched in full" overstates the Semantic Scholar bulk route: the search log honestly notes the bulk endpoint exposes no total hit count, so completeness of retrieval cannot be known, only asserted. The graph route's HTTP 429 is correctly reported in Methods and the log; qualify the bulk route the same way ("retrieved via the bulk route after the graph route returned HTTP 429; the endpoint reports no total, so the 81 records are the retrieved set, not a verified complete set").

4. [Methods, Search methods / PRISMA-S citation searching] Backward chasing is well counted (968 → 269 removed by title triage → 699 screened → 112 assessed → 80 included), but the 269-record title triage — records removed because their titles "name no LLM, chatbot or AI technology" — is described only as "a logged title triage": by whom, how many screeners, and under what rule is not stated in the manuscript, and a title-only technology-term rule will miss records that name a product (e.g. "ChatGPT" is covered, a product-only title without those stems may not be). State the triage's operator and rule in Methods, as PRISMA-S expects for citation searching.

5. [References, throughout] Reference style is below journal standard independent of the Blockers: almost every entry omits the journal name entirely (author, short title, year, DOI only), several titles are truncated (An 2026 omits "…and Prompt Optimization Study"; Miao 2026 and Zuo 2026 titles are shortened), and two otherwise verified entries omit DOIs that exist (Bean 2026 — doi:10.1038/s41591-025-04074-y; Page 2021, PRISMA 2020 — doi:10.1136/bmj.n71). For a JAMIA submission the list must carry full titles, journal, year and DOI for every entry.

6. [PRISMA items 24b/24c; title page and checklist] Registration reporting is honest (no number claimed — a genuine pass), but the protocol itself is only "filed with the review": no public location, DOI or URL is given, so a reader cannot inspect the protocol, the PROSPERO-ready form, or amendments. The checklist also dates the protocol "fixed 12 November 2026," the same day as the search — "before screening" is therefore a same-day claim the package should evidence with the protocol file's own date/version line.

## Closing paragraph
If this paper is published as it stands, what is the worst true thing a critical reader could say about it? That a systematic review whose entire contribution is careful counting — one harm denominator, every count traced — was published with a reference list in which half of a random sample of its citations point somewhere else: DOIs that resolve to a Nature perspective, a 2018 cancer-websites paper, an explainability survey and an oral-surgery article, attached to the very studies whose extracted counts make up the headline 10.5%, including both studies in its era subgroup and the anchor review its chasing was seeded from. The studies are real and, where I could check, their extracted counts match the real papers — which makes it worse, not better: the synthesis may be sound, but the scholarly record as printed does not let any reader verify that, and a reader who clicks one wrong DOI will reasonably doubt every other number in the paper, including the ones that are right. Checklists are promises; this one's citation promise is currently broken, and it is broken in a pattern — DOIs apparently taken from inside other papers' reference lists — that only a full, title-page-level re-verification of all 45-plus references, not a spot fix of the nine named here, can repair.
