## M1 (R7-2). Estimator reporting — **RESOLVED**

The loop-1 remainder was exact and small: two summary surfaces — the Abstract's Authors' conclusions and Conclusions, Implications for practice — still restated the singular "about one in ten" with no estimator range, so the lowest-estimate monopoly survived there in miniature. I specified a one-clause fix in each place. Both clauses are now present, in the form I asked for, with the GRADE grade still attached in the same sentence:

1. **Abstract, Authors' conclusions:** "about one response in ten on the generalised linear mixed model fit (about one in eight on the Hartung-Knapp fit) from a patient-facing LLM chatbot was placed by clinician raters in the study's worst safety tier, with wide variation between question sets (prediction interval 4.5 to 22.7%) and very low certainty."
2. **Conclusions, Implications for practice:** "Rated answers to primary-care questions fall in a study's worst safety tier about one time in ten on the generalised linear mixed model fit (about one time in eight on the Hartung-Knapp fit) on bench sets (very low certainty; across new question sets the share could run from about 5% to about 23%)."

The arithmetic of the plain-language conversions is right: the GLMM fit is 10.5%, about one in ten; the Hartung–Knapp fit is 12.5%, exactly one in eight. Both surfaces now name both estimators rather than printing a bare range, which is stronger than the minimum I set, and they agree with the surfaces I already cleared at loop 1 (Abstract Main results, Summary of Findings, Table 2, Results, Discussion summary, plain language summary). There is now no summary surface in the manuscript where the GLMM figure travels alone. The reporting asymmetry that was this Major is closed, in text and in numbers, not in words only.

**Verdict: Resolved.**

## M4 (R7-5). Narrative set — **PARTLY RESOLVED**

The loop-1 remainder (i) was: no abstract sentence stating the narrative set's main convergent finding, so a reader of the abstract alone learned nothing about what the 121 narrative studies themselves found. Loop 2 (row L2-9) adds one sentence to Abstract, Main results:

> "In the narrative set, studies that checked citations found fabricated references: Khromchenko 2024 found reference problems in 100% of ChatGPT-3.5 references, against 1 of 12 for Bard."

The sentence exists, it is quantified, and its two numbers trace exactly to the source: Khromchenko 2024's abstract (filed at CC005) states that ChatGPT-3.5 "had reference issues in 100% of its references" and Bard "had discrepancies in 8% of its references (one out of 12)". That much is honest transcription. Judged with a sceptic's eye, however, the sentence as a claim about the narrative set is cherry-picked in three specific ways, each checkable in the review's own files:

1. **The lead clause over-labels its own evidence.** The source classifies references as "verified," "broken," "irrelevant," "non-existent" and "no references", and reports the umbrella outcome as reference "issues" or "problems". Only the "non-existent" subset is fabrication. The review's own chasing adjudication says the same thing in so many words: "Reference problems are citation-level (ChatGPT 100% of references with issues), not fabricated medical facts, so not pooled as fabrication", and the extraction notes record that the reference-verification outcome was not extracted as an outcome. The manuscript's quantified half-sentence correctly says "reference problems"; its lead clause upgrades that umbrella to "fabricated references". That is the same move, in miniature, as the original Blocker in this review — a pooled or summarised label asserting more than the underlying category asserts — and I cannot pass it in an abstract sentence I specifically asked for.
2. **One extreme magnitude is presented as a set-level convergence.** The plural "studies that checked citations" rests, in the abstract, on a single quantified study. The body text (Results, Narrative set, finding 2) names three further studies, but without magnitudes: Ucar 2026 and Rao 2026 "report citation hallucination as a measured outcome" (their settled extraction notes likewise qualify it as citation-level, not fabricated medical facts) and McMahon 2024 documents fabricated content "thematically", with no numerator or denominator. So what actually converges across the named studies is the *direction* — citation problems, including hallucinated citations, are found when citations are checked. What does not converge is any magnitude. The abstract selects the single most extreme magnitude in the set, 100%, and lets it stand for the set. The set's other quantified findings do not get this treatment: the disclaimer/referral finding (Reis 2026, 881 of 908, 97%) rests on a far larger denominator, and the four low/no-harm-signal studies named for balance in the body do not reach the abstract at all.
3. **The exemplar's scope and provenance are invisible.** Khromchenko 2024 is a citation-chasing study of 12 commonly asked pregnancy questions put to August-2023 models (ChatGPT-3.5 and Bard) — the oldest models in the evidence base, on a single topic. The abstract sentence carries none of that. Its Bard denominator is given ("1 of 12") while the ChatGPT-3.5 denominator — the number of references behind the 100% — is not stated in the manuscript and is not stated in the source abstract either, so the headline comparison pairs a percentaged whole with a counted fraction. I also note, as a lesser imprecision, that the exemplar is filed in the review's own supplements as a secondary-pool study (supplement-secondary-characteristics.csv; chasing-secondary-settled.csv, problematic tier), not as one of the 121 narrative studies; its citation datum is narrative-level material from a pooled study, which the abstract's "In the narrative set" does not signal.

None of this makes the underlying finding false, and the body section remains, as I graded it at loop 1, honest, counted and balanced. But the Major's point was abstract-level emphasis, and the sentence that was meant to cure the emphasis imbalance introduces a smaller one of its own: the most alarming number available, under a stronger label than its source uses, presented as the convergent finding of 121 studies.

**What remains, exactly:** one sentence, rewritten. Either (a) narrow the sentence to what converges — citation/reference problems, not fabricated references as the umbrella term — name it as one small study with its scope attached (for example: 12 pregnancy questions, 2023 models), and signal that other studies report citation hallucination as an outcome without a comparable magnitude; or (b) replace the exemplar with a sentence that reports the narrative set's findings in the plural form the body actually supports, magnitudes and low-harm signals included. No new analysis and no new data are needed; the material is already in Results, Narrative set. Until that rewrite, the abstract still teaches its reader one thing about the narrative set, and the one thing is the extreme one.

**Verdict: Partly resolved — a fair statement of a real directional finding, cherry-picked in magnitude and over-labelled in its lead clause.**

## M8 (R7-9). Author-position bias — **RESOLVED (as a manuscript item); the deposit/registration step does not block Gate 6**

The loop-1 remainder was the absence of an external anchor: no PROSPERO submission and no timestamped public protocol deposit had been made. The response matrix (R7-9) records that step as barred to the revision agent and reserved to the author. I was asked to state, in my judgement, whether that absence is a manuscript defect that should block Gate 6, or an author submission step that follows the gates by design. My judgement is the second, for four reasons grounded in the protocol and the text:

1. **The gates are pre-submission by design.** PROTOCOL.md places this system "between a finished draft and every external step: preprint deposit, journal submission, arXiv posting". Deposits and submissions are what a Gate 6 pass releases; they are not inputs to it. The Gate 6 checklist in the protocol (Gate 6, Final integrity gate) requires the declarations to be present and accurate, the reporting checklist completed and matching the text, the numbers traced and the references verified. It does not list a completed PROSPERO submission, and PROTOCOL Section 7 states expressly that this protocol "does not replace journal peer review, ethics review, PROSPERO registration, trial registration, or a reporting guideline" — registration sits outside the gates, alongside them.
2. **The manuscript's own undertaking matches that sequence.** Declarations, Data availability states: "Deposit follows the peer-review gates; no DOI has been issued yet", and names the deposit package (including the protocol and the PROSPERO-ready form). The Background carries the matching submission-day commitment for the competitor recheck: "the check will be repeated on any day this review is submitted". The deposit step is therefore not an unkept promise inside the text; it is a dated, declared, post-gate step.
3. **What a manuscript can do about an absent registration, this one does.** Non-registration is declared honestly in five places, with no number claimed or implied: the title block ("The form has not been submitted to PROSPERO and this review is not registered"), the Abstract's Registration line, the Background ("This review is itself unregistered"), Methods, Deviations (6), and the headed Declarations item, Registration. The form itself is on file and verifiable (stage0-protocol/prospero-form.md, headed "NOT submitted"), so Gate 6's fresh agent can check the declaration against the record — which is the verification Gate 6 exists to perform. A manuscript defect would be a false or missing declaration. There is neither.
4. **Blocking Gate 6 on this point would demand a cure that no longer exists.** Deviations (6) states, correctly, that "prospective registration is no longer obtainable for this completed review". A post-hoc submission or a timestamped Zenodo deposit of the protocol package can still create a public, dated record at submission — and should, as an author step on the submission-day checklist, next to the competitor recheck — but it cannot retroactively make this review prospectively registered, and the manuscript's wording will remain "not registered" whatever the author now files. Holding the gate hostage to an impossible status change would punish the honest declaration rather than test it.

The rest of this Major was already cleared at loop 1 and is unchanged in substance: the Discussion section "Author position and the judgements inside this review" names the building activity as a non-financial interest, names the tier mapping, coordinator scope rulings and era cut-off as inside-team decisions with "no PROSPERO registration and no external timestamp to check them against", and points to the filed material that lets a reader re-make each call; the four flagged records (R2022, R2073, R2966, R3051) are stated to remain unread and excluded from every count.

For the record, the condition I attach is procedural, not textual: the submission-day checklist held by the author — not Gate 6 — should carry two dated items, (i) the timestamped public deposit of the review package (the Zenodo deposit the Data availability statement already promises), with its DOI entered in the submission version, and (ii) a recorded decision on submitting the PROSPERO-ready form, with its date and outcome filed. If a target venue requires registration as a condition of submission, that is a venue-fit question for the submission package, not a defect in this manuscript.

**Verdict: Resolved as a manuscript/Gate 5 item. The absence of a PROSPERO submission or timestamped public protocol deposit is an author submission step that follows the gates by design; it should not block Gate 6.**
