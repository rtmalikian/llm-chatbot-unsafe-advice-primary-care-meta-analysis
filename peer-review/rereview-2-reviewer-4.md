## The one point I was asked to judge

### Major 6 — The counted harm is harm as doctors see it, and no patient was in the room when "unsafe" was defined. Status: **Resolved**

What I asked for last time was simple. I asked for two things:

1. One plain sentence in the Discussion itself, as a named limit, saying that no patient or member of the public was involved, and that the harm counted is harm graded by clinicians on paper, not harm as patients felt it. That would make the Methods line true. The Methods line says: "This is named as a limitation in the Discussion."
2. A way for a patient who reads only the Plain Language Summary to learn the same thing.

Both are now done. Here is the evidence, in words any reader can check.

**1. The Discussion now says it.**

The Discussion has a section called "Potential biases in the review process". It lists six points, one by one. The sixth point says, in full:

> "Sixth, no patient or member of the public was involved in the review question, the harm definition or the plain language summary, and the harm counted here is clinician-graded harm on paper, not harm as patients experienced it."

That is the sentence I asked for. It covers all three places where a patient was missing:

- the review question — what the review set out to ask,
- the harm definition — what counts as unsafe,
- the Plain Language Summary — the summary written for patients.

And it says the second half plainly too: the harm in this count is "clinician-graded harm on paper, not harm as patients experienced it". In ordinary words: doctors marked the answers on paper. That is not the same as what a real person felt, understood, or did after reading an answer. The paper now admits that difference in the Discussion, where it said it did.

Because that sixth point is now there, the Methods sentence — "This is named as a limitation in the Discussion" — is now true. Last time it was not. This time I checked, and it is.

This matches what the response matrix says was done in Loop 2, row L2-7.

**2. The Plain Language Summary now says it too.**

Near the end of the Plain Language Summary, in the last paragraph, there is now this sentence:

> "No patient helped decide what counts as unsafe in this review, and no patient read this summary before it was published."

That is plain. A lay reader does not need to guess what it means. If you read only the summary — as many patients will — you now learn two honest facts before you finish it: no patient helped decide what "unsafe" means here, and no patient read the summary written for patients before it went out.

That is what was missing last time for the summary reader, and it is now there.
