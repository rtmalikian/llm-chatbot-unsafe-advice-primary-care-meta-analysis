## Blockers

### Blocker 1 (B-A, tier definition) — RESOLVED. The blocker closes.

I asked for any one of three routes: (a) the verbatim tier label for every primary study in Table 1; (b) a strict-tier re-analysis presented as primary, or an honest relabel of the outcome throughout with the "means the same thing in every contributing study" claim deleted; (c) a sensitivity analysis by label strictness. The revision did (a), the relabel half of (b), and the strict arm of (c), which is the combination the editorial decision (B-A) ruled acceptable. Verified in the text, not the matrix:

- **Tier-label table.** Table 1 now carries a "Verbatim tier label" column for all 24 primary studies, and the full per-arm labels are filed as supplement-tier-labels.csv. The mapping that was previously unauditable can now be checked line by line.
- **Strict-tier sensitivity.** Filed in the revision changelog at 9.47% (95% CI 7.96 to 11.24; 24 arms, 5 studies, 168 of 1,728) and reported in the manuscript at the same values under rounding — 9.5% (8.0 to 11.2) — in the Abstract, the Summary of findings, Results (with the Hartung-Knapp value 10.8% and prediction interval 6.3 to 14.0% beside it) and Table 2, and restated in the Discussion summary. It sits just below the main pool's 10.5%, so the headline does not depend on the loose labels. One coding difference from my Gate 2 count is declared and reconciles exactly: I counted 26 strict arms and 191 events including Buis 2023's two arms; the revision codes Buis separately as triage-discordance (2 arms, 23 events), because its tier is a disposition definition, and 191 − 23 = 168, 26 − 2 = 24. The changelog states the difference in the open and Table 1 labels the study "unsafe (triage)" with a footnote explaining the dual counting. That is a defensible coding, transparently declared — not a silent redefinition.
- **Narrowed outcome wording.** The title now reads "Unsafe or potentially unsafe advice…"; the Abstract Objectives define the outcome as the study's own worst safety tier "where that tier is labelled unsafe or potentially unsafe"; the plain language summary says the worst grade "was called unsafe or potentially unsafe" in most studies; the Background states plainly that the first version's description ("explicitly unsafe, harmful or dangerous in every study") "was too strong", and the "means the same thing in every contributing study" claim is gone. The Abstract now prints the full label breakdown against the headline (24 arms / 168 events asserting unsafe with no qualifier; 55 arms / 327 events, 59.2%, potentially-inclusive; 14 arms / 34 events major-error; 2 arms / 23 events triage-discordance), so a reader meets the heterogeneity in the same paragraph as the pooled number. The Background's differentiation from CRD420261347066 is correspondingly rewritten to the one substantive point (a single harm denominator fixed in advance).

What was pooled is now what the paper says was pooled. The blocker closes.

### Blocker 2 (B-B, era deviation) — RESOLVED. The blocker closes.

I offered two routes; the revision took the second and completed every element of it:

- **Amendment filed.** stage0-protocol/amendment-2026-11-13-era.md is dated 13 November 2026, states in terms that it is retrospective and that it "does not retrospectively make the analysis pre-specified", quotes the protocol's original wording ("GPT-3.5 era versus GPT-4, GPT-4o era and later"), names the calendar re-operationalisation (13 May 2024) and the absence of any pre-analysis amendment, and records the disposition.
- **Demoted to exploratory.** The contrast is removed from the Abstract's findings (no p = 0.001, no 24.3% vs 10.2% billing anywhere in the manuscript — I searched; no interaction p-value survives in the text at all). Results heads the paragraph "Model era is reported here as exploratory and descriptive only", reports the calendar-rule values descriptively (3 arms, 2 studies, 24.1%), names the confounders in the same paragraph (both early studies at high overall risk of bias; one is the emergency triage outlier; the other's responses were edited under a physician-role prompt), and states that "no interaction p-value is carried as a finding". The Objectives now describe item 3 as pre-specified "as a model-generation contrast (see Deviations for how the analysed version departed from it)", and Deviations item (2) discloses the departure in the manuscript itself.

That is the correct accounting: the pre-specified contrast cannot be recovered from this dataset, and the paper now says so instead of borrowing its authority.
- The 4 era-unclassified arms are disclosed wherever era appears (see Minor 1).

One wording residue, recorded for the record and not a ground to hold the blocker: the Objectives still group era under the heading "three pre-specified subgroup contrasts" before the qualifier bites. The qualifier, the Deviations entry and the exploratory Results paragraph together prevent the misreading my blocker targeted. The blocker closes.

## Majors

### Major 1 (poolability judged inconsistently; merged M2) — PARTLY RESOLVED

What is fixed is substantial and verified. A single poolability standard is now stated in Methods (Synthesis): a pooled mean is reported as a result only if it describes the studies that compose it, with the three failure conditions named (prediction interval spanning most of the scale, tier definitions changing meaning, collapse on removing derived-count units). Applied to the problematic tier, the outcome is demoted to study-by-study reporting; the invalid 18.7% (17.2 to 20.2) headline is withdrawn from the Abstract, the Summary of findings (now "not pooled in this version; see text") and the Conclusions, and the "about twice as often" ratio claim built on that pool is deleted. Applied to undertriage, the disowned GLMM mean (14.6%) no longer appears in the Results text; the text states the revision fit does not identify a usable mean.

**What remains, exactly:** the demoted problematic pool's refitted pooled values are still printed in the body of Results (Secondary outcomes): "mixed model 16.3%, Hartung-Knapp 19.3%, prediction interval 1.4 to 72.8%; excluding Khan, 16.9%; excluding the Si units, 14.6%", introduced as "filed in the revision changelog for audit" while in fact appearing in both places. My Gate 2 fix offered the demotion route on the condition that a pooled figure, "if kept at all", live "in the analysis supplement only" — and part of my original complaint was precisely that the disowned undertriage mean was still printed in the Results text. The revision repeats that pattern for the problematic pool: the numbers are framed as describing none of their studies, and the Abstract, Summary of findings and Conclusions are clean, so no headline misleads; but a quotable pooled figure for a pool the paper itself declares unpoolable remains in the main text, and the ex-Khan and ex-Si values are printed without prediction intervals. Moving that one sentence's figures to the changelog/supplement alone (leaving the sentence's audit pointer) closes this Major in full. Until then it is partly resolved.
