# Reviewer report (Gate 2)

Paper: Unsafe medical advice from patient-facing large language model chatbots in primary-care presentations: a systematic review and meta-analysis (Review 4)
Reviewer (persona and bucket): Reviewer 5 — Bucket F. Research ethicist and health-data governance / medical regulatory reviewer, 12 years, university and journal ethics work. Calibrated bias acknowledged: may over-flag cautionary language; the handling editor should weight caution-only items (flagged as such below) accordingly.
Date: 2026-11-13

Materials read in full: manuscript (`stage5-manuscript/manuscript.md`) and `stage0-protocol/prospero-form.md`. Independence: nothing under `review4/peer-review/` was read except `panel-assignment.md`. Remit: PROTOCOL §2.7 in full, §2.11 in full, and the authority-claims part of §2.5. Statistical and estimator questions are outside this remit and are not ruled on.

## Summary (5 to 8 sentences, in your frame)

This is, on its face, an unusually careful safety paper about its own authority: the author framing in the title block is exactly the approved one, no current licence, practice, registration or retired status is claimed anywhere, no PROSPERO number is claimed for this review, and the one registration number that appears is correctly attributed to another team's record. I found no passage that gives individual clinical advice, no dosing, and no medication recommendation to a reader; the medication content is study topics and study results reported in aggregate. The submission-blocking problems in my remit are therefore not in the findings but in the declarations and governance apparatus around them. The ethics statement is a single sentence that covers this review's own conduct and says nothing about the governance basis for the human-user, standardised-patient and forum-derived evidence the review synthesises. The pre-submission peer-review statement required by Rule 1 is absent altogether, the AI-assistance note names no actual tool while the Abstract and Methods describe the review team in unqualified human-sounding role terms, and an absolute "no conflicts / fully independent" declaration sits unreconciled next to the title-block statement that the author is now building clinical AI. A paper whose subject is unsafe advice and unmasked raters should not ship with its own disclosure chain this loose, so I recommend major revision on declarations and governance grounds, with no Rule 10 Blocker found in the clinical or authority content itself.

## Overall recommendation
Major revision. Main reason: no Blocker (see below), but four Majors in §2.7/§2.11 — an incomplete ethics/governance basis for the included human evidence, the missing Rule 1 peer-review statement, an AI-assistance/accountability disclosure that does not identify the tools or the adjudicator, and an unreconciled conflicts declaration — each of which must be resolved on facts only the author can confirm, not by copy-editing alone, before this paper is submitted.

## Blockers
None.

Rule 10 scan, item by item, so the absence is auditable:

1. **Individual clinical advice — not found.** I read the Abstract, Plain language summary, Discussion, and Conclusions/Implications for practice in full for any "you should" addressed to a patient. There is none. Implications for practice (Conclusions) is addressed to "a clinician or service lead", opens "This review makes no recommendation about any chatbot product, and its numbers cannot carry one", and conditions any local decision on "local evaluation on local questions". That is service-level governance commentary inside the data, not individual care advice. Plain language summary closes: "The results describe how chatbots perform on study question sets. They are not a safety rating for any chatbot product a reader can open today."
2. **Medication recommendation — not found.** No drug is recommended to a reader, and no dose, regimen or prescribing instruction appears anywhere. The medication material is (a) topic descriptions ("medication questions in general terms", Methods — Types of questions; "a parent dosing question", Background, as an example of a question type, with no dose given), (b) study topics in the reference/table lists (e.g. antibiotic-prescribing advice, paediatric vitamin D), and (c) a reported study result (Results — Primary outcome: human-user stratum): "inappropriate medication recommendations appeared in 336 of 496 LLM encounters (67.7%…) against 58 of 248 encounters with primary care physicians (23.4%)" — Si 2026's finding, reported critically and in aggregate, with no medication named.
3. **Licence, registration, affiliation or endorsement claim the author does not hold — not found.** Title block, verbatim: "Affiliation: Independent researcher, Palmdale, CA, USA. The author is a healthcare founder and operator who bootstrapped and ran a direct primary care clinic as CEO, now building clinical AI." That is the §2.11 public framing rule word-for-word in substance: past-tense clinic operation as CEO, no current licence or practice, and no retired-status label anywhere in the manuscript. Every other "physician / doctor / clinician" in the text refers to included studies' raters, comparators or titles (e.g. "a single physician rating each response in Draelos 2026"; ""Doctor ChatGPT, Can You Help Me?""), never to the author. No university, employer, journal or regulator affiliation or endorsement is claimed; "follows the RAISE 2025 guidance" (Declarations) is a methods-reporting claim, dealt with as accuracy under Major 3, not an endorsement claim.
4. **Patient-level data without a stated governance basis — not triggered as a Blocker, but see Major 1.** This review itself does not present patient-level data: no individual patient record, quote, screenshot, vignette or forum post is reproduced, and no re-identifiable example appears (checked against §2.7's no-re-identifiable-examples item — Pass on that item). What it synthesises is published, aggregate, arm-level counts. Rule 10's Blocker wording therefore does not bite on the review's own data use; the gap — that this basis is never *stated*, and the included studies' basis is never addressed — is graded Major, not Blocker, and the editor should not let my grading be read as clearance of it.

## Majors
1. [Declarations — Ethics; Results — Primary outcome: human-user stratum; Discussion — Completeness and applicability] **The ethics basis covers only this review's own conduct and gives no governance basis for the human evidence it synthesises.** The entire statement is: "No human participants were involved in this review and no ethics approval was required." Against that, the review includes human-user evidence — Bean 2026 "randomised members of the public", Si 2026's "standardised-patient field experiment" with 496 LLM and 248 physician encounters, and narrative human-user studies of people acting on chatbot advice — and bench evidence whose question sets were, in the review's own words, "written by investigators or lifted from patient forums" (Discussion — Completeness). The manuscript nowhere states the actual basis on which this is unproblematic: that the review accessed only published aggregate results, collected no new data from any person, and accessed no patient-level or identifiable data; whether the included human-user studies' own ethics approval / consent was extracted or checked (the 24-field extraction form as described contains no ethics field, and the adapted risk-of-bias tool's four domains contain no governance item); or on what basis forum-derived question material in primary studies is carried into the synthesis. Read strictly, "No human participants were involved in this review" is also easy to misread against an evidence base that plainly involved human participants. What would fix it: expand the Ethics declaration to (i) scope the sentence to the review's own conduct and give its reason (secondary synthesis of published aggregate data; no new collection; no patient-level or identifiable data accessed); (ii) state, for the human-user studies, the ethics approval / consent position *as reported by those studies*, or state plainly that this was not extracted or checked and carry that as a limitation; and (iii) state the position on forum-derived question sets in the primary studies. If (ii) requires checking the human-user sources, that is verification work, not a wording change.
2. [Declarations — whole section] **The pre-submission peer-review statement is absent, so Rule 1 compliance cannot be verified.** Verbatim search result: **absent**. No sentence in the title block, Abstract or Declarations states that the draft received pre-submission peer review, in any wording. For the record, and so there is no second finding hiding inside this one: nothing in the manuscript calls this process journal peer review, external peer review or independent peer review — the mislabelling Rule 1 prohibits does not occur, because the process is not mentioned at all. The required wording (PROTOCOL Rule 1) is: "The draft received structured multi-agent pre-submission peer review under the author's published protocol. The human author reviewed every comment and is accountable for the final text." What would fix it: add that sentence, exactly, in the Declarations, once the review round it describes has in fact happened — and do not paraphrase it, shorten it, or upgrade it in the cover letter or any post. As printed, this is also the sentence that would carry the author's personal accountability, which Major 3 shows is otherwise left in the passive voice.
3. [Abstract — Data collection and analysis; Methods — Selection of studies / Data extraction / Risk of bias; Declarations — Author contributions and Use of AI assistance] **The AI-assistance disclosure is present but does not identify the tools, and the Abstract and Methods describe an LLM team in unqualified human-sounding role terms, leaving the accountable-human chain unclear.** The AI note as printed, quoted in full: "**Use of AI assistance.** This review was produced with LLM assistance at every stage, and the assistance is part of the method rather than a hidden step. LLM agents screened records under two rule sets, extracted data in duplicate, assessed risk of bias in duplicate, and drafted text, under a protocol fixed before screening. Adjudication of screening disagreements, settlement of extraction conflicts and adjudication of risk of bias ratings were done by reading the underlying sources. The statistical analysis was run in Python and hand-checked by an independent computation route. The author reviewed the settled dataset and this manuscript. This note follows the RAISE 2025 guidance for reporting AI assistance in evidence synthesis: the tools are named by role, the human author of record is named above, and the underlying logs (search log, screening decisions, extraction sheets, settlement record, team log) are filed with the review." Assessment against §2.11: a factual AI-assistance note *is* present, names the author of record, and itemises the roles — that much matches what §2.11 requires in form. It does not match it in substance on three points. (a) "The tools are named by role" is the note's own description of itself, and it is the problem: no LLM system, provider, model version or access dates for the review team are given anywhere in the manuscript — this in a paper that faults its included studies because "model versions and access dates left unreported" (Discussion — Quality of the evidence). The review must meet the standard it scores others against. (b) Outside the Declarations, a reader meets "Two screeners screened all 3,106 deduplicated records apart", "one adjudicator read and settled every disagreement", "Two extractors", "a settlement reader", "Two assessors… an adjudicator settled each one" (Abstract; Methods), with no indication in those sections that every one of those roles was an LLM agent; disclosure that only arrives in the back-matter declarations does not cure methods text that reads as a human team. (c) The adjudication sentences are passive — disagreements "were done by reading the underlying sources", contributions say settlement happened "by reading the source" — and never state *who* adjudicated: the human author personally, or further LLM agents. Under Rule 2 (one accountable human) that distinction is the disclosure, not a detail. The role list is also inconsistent with the PROSPERO-ready form, field 8, which has the LLM team "carrying out search, screening, extraction, risk of bias, statistics, and GRADE roles" — search and GRADE appear there and not in the manuscript note; statistics and drafting are apportioned differently. What would fix it: label the LLM roles as LLM roles at first use in the Abstract and in each Methods subsection; name the system(s), version(s) and dates used for the review work, or state explicitly why they cannot be named; rewrite the adjudication/settlement sentences in the active voice stating who performed them and what the author personally did; and reconcile the role list with PROSPERO form field 8 so the two documents describe the same team.
4. [Declarations — Conflicts of interest, read against the title block] **"None… fully independent" is unreconciled with the author's disclosed clinical-AI building activity.** The declaration as printed: "**Conflicts of interest.** None. The author has no financial or personal interest in any system evaluated in the included studies. The review is fully independent." The title block states the author is "now building clinical AI". Building clinical AI is not, on the face of the paper, an interest in any *evaluated* system — the evaluated systems are third-party general-purpose chatbots, and §2.6's author-built-the-thing test is not met — so I do not allege an undisclosed conflict in a named product. But an absolute "None" plus "fully independent" that never mentions the one adjacent professional activity the paper itself discloses invites exactly the question a journal ethics editor will ask: what is being built, is it commercial, does it compete with or sit downstream of the systems evaluated, and does the author derive income from it? A declaration that pre-empts that question is accurate; one that leaves it to be asked is not yet submission-ready. What would fix it: expand the declaration to address the building activity expressly — its nature and commercial status, whether any product of it is evaluated here or competes with an evaluated system, and any related income or its absence — and let "none" be the conclusion of that sentence rather than a substitute for it. The same reconciliation should be applied to PROSPERO form fields 9–10, which likewise read "none / fully independent" (form field 9: "Funding: none. Fully independent."; field 10: "Conflicts of interest: none.").

## Minors
1. [Title block — Registration line; PROSPERO form field 5] Registration wording is honest but imprecise in two places. The manuscript says: "Registration: no PROSPERO number has been issued for this review. A PROSPERO-ready form was completed before screening and is filed with the review. No registration number is claimed or implied." Checked against the form, which states at its head "NOT submitted. No PROSPERO registration number has been issued for this review, and none is claimed." — the manuscript never says in terms that the form was *not submitted*, and "completed before screening" sits awkwardly against form field 5 ("protocol written, scoping screen in progress, full screening not started") and a form prepared the same day as the search (12 November 2026). Fix: "completed before full screening; the form has not been submitted to PROSPERO and this review is not registered." The competing record is, for the avoidance of doubt, handled correctly throughout: CRD420261347066 is attributed to Okubo and Grein in Background, in Discussion — Agreements and disagreements, and in References, the five differentiation points in Background match the five in the form, and the manuscript even hedges its own negative search ("An empty search result does not show that no publication exists"). Pass on the substance; Minor on the two wordings only.
2. [Plain language summary] Caution-only item, flagged as the kind my persona over-weights: the summary — the one part of the paper a patient is likely to read, in a paper spanning emergency triage, myocardial-infarction education and paediatric burn first aid — contains no explicit "this is not medical advice" / seek-professional-care signpost. No advice is given (see Blocker scan), so this blocks nothing; one closing sentence signposting a reader with a current health concern to professional care would close the loop cheaply.
3. [Declarations — Data availability] The statement is future-tense and unlocated: materials "are filed in the review's stage folders and will be deposited with the review package". No repository, DOI, licence or access route is named, and the governance point within my remit is unstated: the deposited logs (screening decisions, extraction sheets, team log) should be confirmed in the statement to contain study- and arm-level material only, with no identifiable participant or forum-user material. Fix: name the repository/identifier at deposit and add that one confirmation sentence.
4. [Declarations — Funding] Query, not an assertion: "None. This review received no funding from any source." sits beside a review "produced with LLM assistance at every stage". If the tool/compute costs were borne personally by the author, "no external funding; costs borne by the author" would be the more exact sentence and would also support Major 4's reconciliation; if no such costs arose, the present wording stands. The author should confirm which is true rather than leave "any source" doing that work silently.
5. [Conclusions — Implications for practice] Caution/authority wording (§2.5, authority-claims part only): "this review supplies the base rate such an evaluation should be compared against" positions the pooled 10.5% as a comparator for local service decisions in the same section that — correctly — says the numbers "pool study question sets from 2023 to 2026, not products on sale today", on bench-only evidence at GRADE very low with a prediction interval of 3.8 to 26.2%. Qualify it in the same sentence ("the bench-set base rate, not an expected rate in real patient use") so the authority the sentence borrows is visibly the authority the evidence actually has.

## Nits
1. [Title block / Declarations] Registration (Major 2's statement likewise) would survive journal production better repeated as a headed Declarations item than carried only in the title block.
2. [Plain language summary] "doctors or other clinicians read chatbot answers" simplifies the Abstract's "clinician raters" for a lay reader. Acceptable plain-language practice; no change required, noted only so the simplification is a decision on record.
3. [Declarations — Correspondence] "The author welcomes collaboration and correspondence on this review and on clinical AI evidence synthesis." No issue: an invitation to correspond, not an affiliation, credential or endorsement claim.

## Closing paragraph
If this paper is published as it stands, what is the worst true thing a critical reader could say about it?

That a review which scores other researchers down for unreported model versions, unmasked raters and one-sentence safety definitions shipped with a one-sentence ethics statement, an unnamed AI team doing human-titled jobs in its own Methods, an unreconciled "no conflicts", and no statement at all of the pre-submission review it underwent — that its clinical findings are more carefully governed than its own conduct, and that a reader who trusts the rigour of the first will not notice the looseness of the second until a journal's ethics editor does it for them.
